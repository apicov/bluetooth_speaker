# The SBC link, and the two transports it has outlived

The bridge forwards **undecoded SBC over SPI**. It does not decode audio at all.

```
phone ──A2DP/SBC──▶ bt_bridge ──SBC over SPI──▶ hub_s3 ──▶ DAC + LEDs
                    no decode     4 wires          decodes here
```

**Status: live.** Cleared the UART's bar on first hardware: sustained
`eff ~43.7–44.2 kHz` against 44,100 nominal (the UART did ~44,050), with
`crc 1–2 short 1–2 gaps 1–2` per window — at the UART's own residual floor
(`0–2 bad sync, 0–1 CRC errors`), not zero. The bring-up's first fault was
physical, exactly as step 3 below predicts: a marginal CS/data jumper that the
`short` detector localised and reseating the wire cleared. `crc` is the counter
that says when the wiring is outpaced as the clock is raised — and the wiring
has since cleared it clean all the way to 10 MHz, so the bitpool cap of 53 (the
UART's honest limit) is now the last ceiling to lift.

> `hub/`, the classic ESP32 hub, still speaks the UART and **can no longer
> receive anything from the bridge**. See
> [`hub-s3-gap-list.md`](hub-s3-gap-list.md) §7.

## Why the link moved off the UART

Not quality, and not corruption. Capacity.

| | |
|---|---|
| UART at `SBC_LINK_BAUD` = 500000 8N1 | **50 kB/s, hard, no flow control** |
| Measured payload | **~42 kB/s** — 50 packets/s of ~830 bytes |
| Utilisation | **84%** |

500000 is not where the protocol stops. It is where breadboard jumper leads
start producing bad sync bytes:

| Baud | Result, per 5 s window |
|---|---|
| 1,000,000 | ~50% of packets corrupt |
| 750,000 | 20–30 bad sync, 15–20 CRC errors |
| **500,000** | **0–2 bad sync, 0–1 CRC errors** |

Packets are ~830 bytes, so a bit error rate of only 0.013% loses half of them —
large packets amplify a small BER.

The consequence was paid in audio quality. The SBC endpoint advertised
`max_bitpool = 250`, roughly five times what the wire could carry, and nothing
enforced the difference: a phone choosing bitpool 76 asked for ~59 kB/s down a
50 kB/s link, and the surplus was not slowed down, it was dropped. Whether that
happened depended on which handset connected. `max_bitpool` came down to 53 —
the honest statement of the wire, and 85% of it.

**SPI is what lets that go back up.** 1 MHz is 125 kB/s, 20×. 10 MHz is
1.25 MB/s, 25×. Past that the bitpool stops being a wire question.

## Why not I2S, and why SPI is not the same mistake

The first design sent decoded PCM over I2S, bridge as master and hub as slave.
It silently lost **~1.15% of frames**, and finding out why took longer than
anything else in the project. Almost every intuition was wrong:

| Suspect | Ruled out by |
|---|---|
| Bridge clock wrong | Logic analyser: WS exactly 44,100 Hz |
| I2S driver broken | Self-loopback on one board: zero loss over 45 s |
| WiFi / DMA bandwidth | Loopback with no WiFi loses identically |
| Visualiser / CPU load | 577/s before, 577/s with it disabled |
| Read scheduling | Max read gap only 0.7% above average — no stalls |
| WS signal integrity | Hardware edge counter: 44,099, exact |
| BCLK ringing | Edge-ratio counter: 32.000, exact |
| Grounding | Extra ground wires: only 12% better |
| **Asynchronous slave clocking** | **Inverting clock ownership: loss to zero** |

**The ESP32's I2S slave receiver drops frames when sampling a clock generated by
a different chip's crystal.** Everything on the wire was perfect; the peripheral
did not deliver what arrived at its pins. Inverting ownership fixed the loss and
broke bit alignment instead — the slave then has to close a master-clocks,
slave-drives, master-samples round trip inside half a bit period, which does not
happen across two boards. The fix relocated the problem.

UART sidestepped the category entirely: each byte resynchronises on its own
start bit, so **no clock is shared**.

SPI shares a clock. That is the obvious objection to this change and it deserves
a straight answer. The two are clocked differently:

- **I2S is continuous.** The slave tracks a free-running foreign clock for ever
  and has nothing to realign against. Alignment error accumulates and there is
  no boundary at which it is discarded.
- **SPI is transactional.** CS frames every transfer and the bit counter resets
  at each assertion. Nothing accumulates across frames, so the worst a clock
  difference can cost is one frame — and the CRC catches that one.

**That is an argument, not a measurement.** `crc` in the `sbc_in` line is what
tests it and is expected to sit at 0. If it does not, this section is what was
wrong.

## Wiring

Four signals and a common ground. Bridge is master, hub is slave.

| bt_bridge (ESP32) | hub_s3 (XIAO ESP32-S3) | |
|---|---|---|
| GPIO 14 — SCK | GPIO 44 (D7) | HSPI IOMUX on the bridge |
| GPIO 13 — MOSI | GPIO 6 (D5) | |
| GPIO 15 — CS | GPIO 5 (D4) | the framing |
| GPIO 25 — HANDSHAKE in | GPIO 3 (D2) — out | hub says "buffer armed" |
| GND ×4 | GND ×4 | keep them all |

No MISO — the link is one-way, and not claiming HSPI's MISO leaves GPIO 12
alone, which is the MTDI strapping pin that must be low at boot.

Two pins carry history. **GPIO 25** was the UART TX and **GPIO 44** the UART RX,
so two of the four leads were already run.

**What this cost.** GPIO 5 was `DANCEFLOOR_MONITOR_GPIO`. The marker/monitor
instrument needs GPIO 4 *and* GPIO 5, so it is finished on this board — the four
SPI signals took all but one free pad on an eleven-pad module. It was the
cheapest of the available prices: the instrument is off by default, needs a wire
between two boards that a deployed floor cannot have, and nothing corrects on
it. `TRACK DIVERGENCE` over WiFi still covers every satellite. The classic hub
would not have to pay this, having pins to spare — see
[`hub-s3-gap-list.md`](hub-s3-gap-list.md) §7.3.

**Not GPIO 43.** The console lives there, moved off USB deliberately so
`ESP_PHY_ENABLE_USB` could be disabled: the S3's USB and WiFi PHYs interfere and
IDF's mitigation is to power USB down.

## The handshake, which is not optional

ESP-IDF's `spi_slave` **loses any transfer the master clocks with no transaction
queued.** The peripheral has nowhere to put it. There is no buffering, no error,
and nothing at either end that can distinguish the result from corruption.

So the hub raises a line while it has a buffer armed and drops it for the
duration of a transfer, and the bridge does not start one until it is high.

- The hub keeps **two** DMA buffers queued. One is armed while the other is
  decoded in place, so the line is high except during a transfer — a decode
  takes far longer than a transfer does, and a single buffer would leave the
  bridge waiting through every one.
- The bridge waits on a **GPIO interrupt and a task notification**, never a
  poll. That chip runs Bluetooth Classic, and a task spinning on a pin is what
  that budget cannot absorb. The common path costs one GPIO read.
- The line is **pulled down** on the bridge, so a hub that is off, reflashing or
  crashed reads as "not ready" and shows up as a stall count rather than a
  stream clocked into nothing.

## Wire format

```c
struct __attribute__((packed)) {
    uint8_t  kind;        /* LINK_KIND_SBC = 0, LINK_KIND_META = 1 */
    uint8_t  rsv;
    uint16_t len;         /* payload bytes that are real */
    uint32_t seq;         /* assigned at ENQUEUE, see below */
    uint16_t crc;         /* CRC-16/CCITT over header + payload, crc zeroed */
    uint16_t rsv2;
} spi_link_hdr_t;         /* 12 bytes, then 1024 of payload */
```

Defined once in `components/dancefloor_sync/include/sbc_link.h`; both ends
include it and must agree.

**No sync bytes.** CS is the framing — one assertion is one packet. The UART
header carried `0xA5 0x5A` and the receiver scanned for them because a byte
stream has no boundaries of its own; the resync scan, the straddling-header
case, the false-sync branch and the `sync` counter all existed to serve that,
and all of them are gone. That is about eighty lines of receiver deleted.

**Every transaction is a fixed 1036 bytes, padded.** Both ends then agree on the
length *before* the transfer, so nothing depends on the slave reporting how many
bits it actually received — a wrong `trans_len` is indistinguishable from
corruption. It costs ~25% of the wire at bitpool 53, against 20–25× headroom.
The size is also what satisfies the SPI slave's DMA rule that the buffer be
4-byte aligned with a length that is a multiple of 4, which is why the header is
12 bytes and not 11.

**CRC-16/CCITT replaces the XOR byte**, and the reason is the sync words
leaving. On the UART a corrupt `len` was survivable because the scan would
eventually find the next real sync pair; here nothing else checks it. An 8-bit
XOR is a parity per bit column and misses any even number of flips in one of
them — `test_sync_proto.c` case 20 builds exactly that frame, confirms the old
checksum accepts it, and confirms the CRC does not.

**`seq` is assigned when the packet is queued, not when it is transmitted.** The
first version numbered at transmit time, so a packet dropped by a full queue
never got a number and the hub saw an unbroken sequence — drops were completely
invisible, and `gaps 2` was reassuring and meaningless. A transport rewrite is
exactly the change that would silently revert this.

## Three bugs that each looked like a link fault

Kept because none of them was a link fault, and each cost a transport-level
investigation that came back innocent.

**Byte-at-a-time resync.** The receiver hunted for the sync word with one
`uart_read_bytes()` call per byte. After a CRC error it rescanned ~660 bytes of
a corrupt packet that way — tens of thousands of driver calls per second,
backing up the RX buffer and causing further corruption. Errors fed errors. It
went to a bulk read into a 4 kB buffer, and with CS framing the whole mechanism
no longer exists.

**Undersized input buffer.** `streamer_feed()` drops silently when full, and one
A2DP packet decodes to ~3.5 kB of PCM in a single burst against an 8 kB buffer.
Audio was being discarded *after* a link that measured perfectly clean. Now
32 kB, and `fed-drop` counts it.

**Over-eager stream-stop detection.** A 100 ms gap in the input was treated as
"stream ended", which restarts the presentation timeline *and empties the
playback ring*. SBC arrives in bursts and occasional multi-packet gaps exceed
100 ms, so this fired during ordinary playback and threw away ~200 ms of good
audio each time. Now 500 ms.

The common thread: each produced symptoms indistinguishable from a bad link, and
each was found only by adding a counter for the specific thing being lost.

## Reading the log

```
I sbc_in: pkts 252 | 44100 Hz x2 | eff 44050 Hz | hdr 0 crc 0 short 0 gaps 0
          dec 0 dcrc 0 | fed-drop 0 B | max gap 100189 us
```

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pkts` | packets accepted in the window; ~250 per 5 s is normal |
| `44100 Hz x2` | read from the SBC frame header, not measured or inferred |
| `eff` | **PCM samples/s actually reaching playback** — the number that matters |
| `hdr` | frames refused on an impossible `kind` or `len`, before the CRC ran |
| `crc` | link integrity. **This is the counter that tests the argument above** |
| `short` | a transfer that did not arrive whole (`trans_len` short of a full frame): CS split or merged one. Zero means every `crc`/`hdr` failure is a bit error inside a complete frame, not a framing break |
| `gaps` | packets the bridge dropped, visible because `seq` is set at enqueue |
| `dec` | SBC decode failures other than the frame's own CRC |
| `dcrc` | SBC frames whose own CRC-8 failed despite the link CRC-16 passing — corruption past the link check |
| `fed-drop` | PCM discarded because the input buffer was full; must be 0 |
| `max gap` | longest silence between audio packets. 79–112 ms is normal — the source is bursty. Past 150 ms the window prints at once |

`hdr` was called `sync` on the UART and counted bytes skipped hunting for a sync
word. There is no such thing on this link, so a column that could only ever
print 0 was renamed rather than kept — an instrument with nothing behind it is
worse than no column.

`eff` is the honest health metric. Everything else can look perfect while audio
quietly goes missing further down.

On the bridge, three counters and each prints only when it moves:

```
W sbc_spi: queue full: 12 dropped (+12)
W sbc_spi: payload past the link ceiling: 3 dropped (+3)
W sbc_spi: hub never raised the handshake: 1 dropped (+1)
```

**`queue full` is the one that has never been read**, and it answers a question
that is still open — see below.

## Bringing it up

The order matters, and each step is one variable.

1. **Read the bridge console across a track change, before anything else.** A
   1.76 s hole in the SBC input was seen at a track start (`max gap 1760401
   us`), cascading into a hub timeline restart displaced 338 ms and a satellite
   refusing 237 consecutive packets. **SPI will not fix that if it is the
   phone.** The bridge cannot buffer that long — the 8 kB TX buffer was 164 ms
   and the queue ~430 ms — so it is either the source stopping or the queue
   dropping everything, and `queue full: N dropped` says which. That log has
   never been read.
2. **Run at `DANCEFLOOR_SBC_LINK_SPI_HZ` = 1 MHz** across at least one track
   change. Looking for: `hdr 0 crc 0 short 0 dcrc 0`, `gaps` no worse than the
   UART's, `max gap` back to the 110–150 ms burst pattern, `eff` ~44050,
   `fed-drop 0`, and no bridge counter moving at all. `short` is the one that
   names a wiring fault's shape: non-zero means CS is splitting transfers
   (framing), zero-with-`crc` means SCK/MOSI bit errors (signal integrity). If
   `crc`/`hdr` move here, drop the clock (step 3) before anything else.
3. **Move the clock** — `DANCEFLOOR_SBC_LINK_SPI_HZ`, reflash the **bridge**
   only. If step 2 was not clean, go the other way first: 500 k, then 250 k. If
   `crc`/`hdr` fall toward 0 as the clock drops, the fault is confirmed physical
   (jumpers / grounds / SCK edge rate), not code. Once a speed is clean, raise
   it — 2, 5, 10 MHz — confirming step 2 at each. These are the same breadboard
   jumpers that set the UART's ceiling, and SPI adds a clock line to the bundle.
   Expect wiring to be the limit again, and keep every ground wire.
4. **Only then raise `max_bitpool`** above 53 in `bt_bridge/main/main.c`. The
   new constraint is `SBC_LINK_MAX_PAYLOAD` at 1024 bytes against ~830 today,
   and `payload past the link ceiling` is the counter that says when packets
   have outgrown it rather than the link outgrowing them.

Changing the transport and the bitpool together would give any fault two
candidate causes, which is how this project produced most of its confident wrong
answers.

## Done since

The hub used to decode and send **PCM** over WiFi, carrying 1.4 Mbps. It now
forwards the SBC undecoded and each satellite decodes for itself, quartering the
airtime. Measured, PCM cost ~7% of packets rejected by a full transmit queue plus
~13% lost on the air; both went away.

The over-the-air side is described in [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) §9.
